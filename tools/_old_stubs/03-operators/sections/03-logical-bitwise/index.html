Chapter 03 · Section 3.3

Logical and bitwise operators

Some symbols look similar but answer different kinds of questions. A beginner’s first job is to notice whether a symbol appears once or twice and not to guess its behavior from appearance alone.

Vocabulary-confirmed · semantics need tests

Single and doubled marks

&    |    ^    ~    !
&&   ||

The lexer has separate names for the single-character marks and the doubled && and || forms. That distinction matters: tokenization sees them as different symbols, not as two adjacent copies of a single token.

Logical questions

In many C-family languages, doubled ampersand and pipe combine truth-valued expressions. Coral’s token names And and Or map to those spellings. Whether the operators short-circuit, which means the second side may not be evaluated, is a behavioral guarantee that must be tested.

Bitwise operations

Single ampersand, pipe, and caret are commonly used to combine individual bits; tilde commonly inverts bits. That is the conventional C-family expectation, but the accepted operand types and precise Coral semantics should be confirmed against compiler tests.

Why the distinction matters

A boolean-like question and a bit-mask calculation may use related punctuation but are not interchangeable operations. If the left side is a boolean, the compiler may treat a spelling differently than if it is an integer. Let the types and compiler diagnostics settle the question; do not choose an operator by visual similarity alone.

Test plan

  1. Compile a boolean expression using each doubled form.
  2. Use side-effecting functions to test whether the right side is skipped.
  3. Compile small integer bit-mask examples using the single forms.
  4. Record which operand and result types the checker accepts.
